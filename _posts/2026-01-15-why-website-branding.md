It has been years since I actually built a website. I came up in the era of the first browser wars, when your biggest design decision was whether to bold the font or blink it. Then CSS arrived, then e-commerce engines, then persona tracking platforms like Sitecore. My career drifted toward back-office technologies, and I stopped thinking about front-end work entirely.

But here's the problem with spending 25 years in enterprise automation: most of what you accomplish gets filtered through NDAs and proprietary technology. Your best work becomes invisible. You can talk about the outcomes, but the substance belongs to someone else.

So I made a decision. It is time to decouple my personal identity from my corporate identity.

## Treating Yourself Like a Product

The key to any product development, including yourself, is setting clear target goals. What are you trying to accomplish?

My current target is reaching expert-level proficiency in terminal-based AI workflows. I believe the landscape will look completely different by 2028, but that's a topic for a future post.

You might ask what terminal AI has to do with building a website. Fair question. Here's the connection: it's the same product management discipline I've applied for decades. You define the destination, break it into discrete phases, and adapt along the way.

My roadmap looks something like this:

- **Build an online presence and establish a market differential.** Know what to share and what to keep private.
- **Get organized first.** Local files, notes, and knowledge management have to be in order before anything goes public. That means investing in tools like a Synology NAS, Directory Opus, and Obsidian vaults.
- **Upskill deliberately.** Learn Markdown deeply. Be able to describe the practical differences between AI platforms. Combine those tools in the terminal to automate the repetitive copy-and-paste workflows.
- **Approach it with a security mindset.** Run local as much as possible. Any cloud-based tools must be isolated with human controls.
- **Stay cost-conscious.** Avoid long-term subscription fatigue. Every recurring cost needs to justify itself.
- **Plan for legacy.** How does this knowledge transfer to the next generation?

That last one catches people off guard. But if you're building a personal knowledge system, the question of what happens to it after you matters - especially when your legacy is not rooted in applied technology.

## The Point

None of this is complicated. It's just discipline. The same phased approach that works for enterprise product launches works for reinventing your professional presence.

Define the target. Break it into phases. Adapt along the way.

The website is just the first deliverable. The real product is the system behind it.
