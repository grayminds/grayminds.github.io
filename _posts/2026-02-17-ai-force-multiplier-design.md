What differentiates a person, a product, or a company in 2026? **_It is not AI._**

<span class="concept-highlight">AI is a force multiplier.</span>

In capable hands it reshapes organized work, just as earlier multipliers did: Roman road engineering, the medieval heavy plow and stirrup, the Renaissance printing press, the Industrial Age steam engine, the Modern Age adoption of electricity, and the Information Age transistor.

Human society existed before AI and will exist after it. The impact is practical, not apocalyptic. AI amplifies what already exists. A force multiplier allows the same number of people to produce greater outcomes by expanding effective power, productivity, and coordination.

<span class="concept-highlight">The differentiator is not the technology. It is the human using it.</span>

AI increases output, but it does not improve judgment. It does not understand context, intention, or consequence. Those come from people.

## Design Determines Whether AI Actually Works

<P>Good design determines whether a system can actually be used.  As [Don Norman](https://a.co/d/05G9J4o2) explains, systems fail not because users are incompetent, but because the design does not communicate how the system works.  AI systems are especially vulnerable to this.  Early computing captured the same lesson in the phrase "garbage in, garbage out."  When assumptions, data quality, and process safeguards are weak, the output may be precise yet wrong.</P>

<P>AI and ML systems do not understand truth.  They detect patterns and predict outcomes.  Biased, incomplete, mislabeled, or contextually incorrect data produces confident errors.  A large portion of my career has involved troubleshooting technology implementations where the real problem was not the software but the gap between system behavior and human expectation.</P>

<P>An organization may deploy powerful models, yet if outcomes confuse users, especially users who question results, trust does not form.  This becomes even more critical when agentic systems operate autonomously without human judgment.  At that point the value collapses.  The problem is not the model.  The problem is the human experience around the model.</P>

## AI is Quietly Improving Design

<P>A positive side effect of the current AI investment cycle is renewed attention to design.  Agents, agentic workflows, MCP, and even the return of the CLI increase transparency.  They reveal the actions a system actually allows and the clues showing how to interact with it.  In many ways we are moving beyond the limitations of the purely mouse-driven UI toward interfaces that better express system capability.</P>

Don Norman's [_The Design of Everyday Things_](https://a.co/d/05G9J4o2) influenced me years ago, and it remains highly relevant for anyone building products or technology.

Design, however, is not sufficient by itself. The critical element is still the human who connects other humans to interactive systems. Not the IQ of model builders, but the EQ of the people guiding how AI is used as a multiplier.
