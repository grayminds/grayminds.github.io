## Cognitive Systems Engineer

Before I built automation products, I studied how people work with automated systems.  My graduate research at Georgia Tech's Center for Human-Machine Systems Research focused on an intelligent tutoring system for pilot training:  translating expert knowledge into rule-based decision systems, and defining where adaptive instruction should step in.  It was early AI model design, framed by human factors.

That foundation shapes every automation decision I make now.  The question is never just "can the model do this?"  It is "where does the model earn trust, and where does a human need to stay in the loop?"

### Where it shows up

- **Confidence-gated processing.**  I establish accuracy thresholds per field type, routing high-risk extractions to human review and low-risk fields to automation, so accuracy and user trust both improve.
- **Training-data and evaluation strategy.**  Edge-case failures and downstream error costs decide what gets automated, not a demo that worked once.
- **Human-machine systems thinking.**  I map where people stumble in a process and make the next step obvious, whether that process is a document workflow or a fight scene on a stage.

### Published research

My work on intelligent tutoring systems for aviation automation, with researchers including Christine M. Mitchell, was published in 2000 and 2001.  The papers are listed on my [publications](/publications/) page.

The through-line from that research to today's agentic systems is one conviction:  automation should augment human judgment, not quietly replace it.  More on how that informs my product work is on the [Technologist](/technologist/) page.
